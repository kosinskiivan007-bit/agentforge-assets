AgenC: a live agent marketplace under $1M — Mycelium candidate research

The Mycelium Strategy connects selected Solana projects through shared liquidity pools; more than 84% of the tradable $MYC supply is already at work in those pools, and trading fees help fund new connections. I screened sub-$1M Solana tokens for a product people can use today, real volume and liquidity, at least 60 days of trading, recent activity, and no existing place in the Mycelium basket. What AgenC is

AgenC is a protocol and SDK for launching agent marketplaces. agenc.ag is the live reference marketplace: a buyer posts a one-off task or hires a standing service, SOL is escrowed on-chain, an AI agent claims and delivers the work, and the Solana program settles the split. The documented fee model is a 5% protocol fee with a four-leg split: the worker gets at least 60%, the protocol 5%, the marketplace operator up to 20%, and the referrer up to 20%. The site serves agent builders, agent operators, and buyers who need marketing, research, copywriting, finance, or media work.

Facts: the product is not a mock. The public stats endpoint reported 564 settled tasks, 256 registered agents, 85 visible services, 39 completed service hires, 13 unique completed buyers, and a 69.2% repeat-purchase rate among those buyers. The sample is small, but the direction is real. The project also won a pump.fun Build in Public hackathon and publishes a public SDK and CLI.

My interpretation: AgenC is the strongest sub-$1M candidate I found because the marketplace has actual settlement history and a clear buyer/agent loop. The product is the reason to pay attention.

The token is the weak link

I found no official page that gives $AgenC a protocol role. The documented fees are paid in SOL, not in $AgenC. The project's February 2026 X post said 375M AgenC (37.5% of supply) was locked on Streamflow, but the on-chain picture is less clean: the largest account is a plain system wallet holding 38.15%, and a separate Streamflow Vault holds 7.29%. I could not map the 375M statement to a specific vesting contract, and I found no published unlock schedule. That is a material unresolved question, not a reason to pretend the token has utility it has not documented.

Holder concentration is the second issue. After excluding the verified PumpSwap AMM base vault D8RYtb57B3UmEJEGmQ4DPP7SBpGVkzaxQPLixyFLHjB4 (8.47%) and the Meteora DLMM pool 4vcJfHLqe6Erj2huazg2qz2dMp47pcSiyL3iWQkHPRLX (0.80%), the ten largest remaining accounts hold 54.47% of supply; the calculation source is the Rugcheck top-holders report. The 38.15% wallet is unlabeled; the 7.29% Streamflow Vault is identifiable as a locker. There was no burn account in the top 20. Rugcheck scored the token 37/100 with a single-holder warning. Mint and freeze authorities are both revoked, and the main PumpSwap LP is 100% locked; the Meteora DLMM positions are NFT-based, so Rugcheck reports 0% locked and that portion is unverifiable from the report.

Mycelium fit

Collaboration is the best fit. Mycelium could post research, community, or bounty work through AgenC and use its escrow and settlement rail, or run a small pilot that brings Mycelium's community into the agent marketplace. That is a concrete integration, not an existing partnership.

A MYC/AgenC liquidity pair is possible but secondary. AgenC already has most of its liquidity in PumpSwap and Meteora; another pair would fragment it, and the token has no documented fee capture to make the pair compelling.

A holding thesis is weak today. The product is real, but the token does not yet have a documented link to protocol revenue. I would not recommend a treasury allocation until the 38.15% wallet and any fee-sharing or unlock plan are clarified. A collaboration pilot is the lower-risk way to build a relationship.

One promising aspect: a live marketplace with repeat buyers and on-chain settlement is rare under $1M. One meaningful risk: a single unlabeled wallet holds 38.15% and the token's utility is undocumented, so product success may not accrue to $AgenC holders.
